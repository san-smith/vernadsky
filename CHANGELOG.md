## [Unreleased]

### Added

- The land hypsometry in the export contract (params version 6; the reader still accepts versions 0–5 and rejects unknown ones): `land_hypsometry` (domain 1–4, default 1) in `TerrainParams` and `TerrainConfig` bends the land elevation curve, `meters = (2(n − 0.5))^k · 6000` above the sea level mark. The linear curve put more than half of the land above 1500 m at every land share, where the lowland factor zeroes the natural potential and the lapse rate freezes the climate; about `3` matches Earth's hypsometric curve — most land in the lowlands under a few high ranges — and leaves 16–19 % of the land above 1500 m. The curve is monotonic and keeps every land cell at least a meter above the sea, so the coast, the water, and the land structure stay where they are. A committed params-v5 export fixture pins legacy readability.

- The terrain recipe surface in the export contract (params version 5; the reader still accepts versions 0–4 and rejects unknown ones): `land_ratio` (domain 0.05–0.95) and `island_density` (domain 0–3) in `TerrainParams` and `TerrainConfig`, constants of the stage before (`0.3`, `0.5`); the readers of the older layouts synthesize them. A committed params-v4 export fixture pins legacy readability. The land-share search refines its coarse step when the coarse step misses the target by more than 1.5 percentage points (`LAND_RATIO_TOLERANCE`), so the land share meets any target across the domain at any grid.
- Water territories by target area: `WaterSplit::{Count, CellsPerTerritory}` and `generate_with` in `vernadsky-territory`, `build_world_with` in `vernadsky-tools`, and a `--water-cells` flag in the tools' `render` and `dump` subcommands. The count follows the water area (at least one territory per connected water component, at most one per cell) and the largest-remainder apportionment distributes it; `generate` with a count is unchanged, and land identifiers never depend on the water split.
- `land_components` in `vernadsky-territory`: the territory counts of the land components, largest first, without thresholds; the tools' `validate` prints the land share and the components.
- A release-resolution structure test (`vernadsky-tools`, `tests/recipe_structure.rs`, ignored by default and run by CI in release): 27 recipe-surface combinations at 512² and 1024² over three seeds — the land share within tolerance, the water territory area within 15 % of its target, and the largest land component's resolution drift within 10 points on average over the seeds.

### Changed

- **Breaking for world content hashes** (managed drift, parameters only): the stages write params version 6, so the export carries the land hypsometry. With the default linear curve every cell, territory, region, water body, and river is identical to params version 5 — verified on the golden seeds, both erosion profiles, and 128²–512² worlds; the terrain, territory, and export manifests were regenerated for the parameter block.
- **Breaking for world content hashes** (managed drift, parameters only): the stages write params version 5, so the export of the same seed and parameters carries the two new terrain fields. With the default values every cell, territory, region, water body, and river is identical to params version 4 — verified on the golden seeds, both erosion profiles, and 128²–512² worlds. The full-pipeline golden manifests (terrain, territory, export) were regenerated through their documented procedures; the synthetic core, climate, biome, and hydrology manifests are untouched.

- **Breaking for world content hashes** (managed drift): the terrain smoothing is seamless along x. The horizontal pass of the box blur clamped its window at the map edges, so the two edges were averaged over different neighbourhoods and the relief broke at the x seam even though the noise, the island lift, and the erosion all wrap around the cylinder. The window now wraps (a row narrower than the window averages the whole row once); the vertical pass still clamps at the poles. On 256×256 worlds the mean height jump across the seam falls to the level of any interior column pair (seed 42: 159 → 115 m against 96 m inside; seed 7: 114 → 69 m against 78 m). The parameters and their layout are unchanged (params version 4); the full-pipeline golden manifests (terrain, territory, export) were regenerated through their documented procedures, and the synthetic core fixtures are untouched.
- **Breaking for world content hashes** (managed drift): the terrain noise is map-space normalized — the base and island feature counts are fixed fractions of the map circumference (`features_across`, default `1.5`) instead of a per-cell frequency — so every grid resolution renders the same planet layout with proportionate detail, and the world stays a playable continent-plus-islands map instead of dissolving into an archipelago as the grid grows. The largest land component now holds ~91% of the land at every scale (up from 7.8% at 2048×2048), and the octave stack is capped by a detail floor (`min_feature_cells`, default `8`) so sub-floor speckle never exists. Full-pipeline golden manifests (hydrology, terrain, territory, export) were regenerated through their documented procedures; the synthetic core fixtures are untouched.
- **Breaking for world content hashes** (previous managed drift): the river drainage threshold is resolution-independent — per land component, `max(8.0, river_land_share × component land cells)` instead of a fixed 8 cells for the whole world — so every landmass carries a river network proportional to its own size, and the network stays thin and branching at every grid resolution instead of thickening into blobs as the grid grows. The calibrated default share is `0.12%`. Full-pipeline golden manifests (hydrology, terrain, territory, export) were regenerated through their documented procedures; the synthetic core fixtures are untouched (the floor governs tiny worlds).

### Added

- Map-space terrain parameters in the export contract (params version 4; the reader still accepts versions 0–3 and rejects unknown ones): `features_across` and `min_feature_cells` in `TerrainParams`, with TOML fields in `TerrainConfig` and a `--terrain-config` flag in the tools' `render` and `dump` subcommands.
- `HydrologyParams` in the export contract (params version 3, block-presence flags bit 2; the reader still accepts versions 0–2 and rejects unknown ones) carrying the river land share, with `HydrologyConfig` TOML loading in the hydrology crate and a `--hydrology-config` flag in the tools' `render` and `dump` subcommands. A committed params-v2 export fixture pins legacy readability.

### Added

- Export format v1 in `vernadsky-core`: the header now carries the biome registry version (`biomes-v1`) alongside the schema, identifier strategy, and lattice registry versions, making the file self-describing for biome interpretation. The reader accepts schema v0 and v1 files (the v0 layout stays readable) and rejects unknown schema, registry, and params versions; a checked-in v0 fixture pins legacy readability. The tools' `validate` subcommand prints a file summary with exit codes, and `dump` takes grid dimensions and territory counts with pipeline timing.
- Derived suitability features in a new `vernadsky-export` crate: enrichment fills the starting natural potential of every land territory (temperature comfort × moisture × lowland factor, averaged per territory on the 0…1000 permille lattice), and neutral geographic facts — coastal exposure, river mouths reaching an ocean or a sea, and mountainous terrain per the biome registry — are available as deterministic queries for consumers to interpret as ports, estuaries, and passes. The tools' new `potential` layer renders the gradient.
- Region grouping in `vernadsky-territory`: continents and sea basins as connected components of the territory adjacency graph (same-surface 4-neighborhood, x-seamless, poles clamped) via petgraph `UnionFind`; region identifiers anchor at the smallest member territory anchor, and every territory references exactly one region. The tools' new `regions` layer renders the coarse zones.
- Territory partition stage in a new `vernadsky-territory` crate: the mapgen partition ported — land candidates weighted by the actual climate and height lattices (quantized onto the `SeedWeight` lattice with canonical tie-breaks), seeds sampled evenly across the weight spectrum, multi-source BFS growth, and nearest-centroid fill. Water is partitioned per connected component: every lake carries at least one territory, large components split by the largest-remainder rule, so the sea is never one undivided space. Territory identifiers anchor at the final centroid through the stable identifier strategy — land first, then water — and the counts are generation arguments (`land_count`, `water_count`). The stage consumes no randomness. The tools' pipeline now partitions the world, so the `territory` layer shows the real land and sea partition.
- Terrain stage in a new `vernadsky-terrain` crate: the mapgen heightmap pipeline ported — fBm OpenSimplex2 noise on the x-seamless cylinder, island effect, smoothing, power correction, min/max normalization with land-ratio targeting, physical hand-off in meters (sea level `0.5 ⇔ 0 m`, ±6000 m scale) — plus an optional erosion profile (thermal talus slide and hydraulic droplets from the `terrain.erosion` stream) configurable through TOML. `skeleton_world(width, height, seed)` in `vernadsky-core` is the dimension-parameterized entry of the real pipeline. Params version 2 introduces block-presence flags (climate and terrain blocks) in the export, read back compatibly. Golden manifests pin both generation profiles — with and without erosion — over the full real pipeline (terrain → climate → biomes → hydrology). The tools now render the real pipeline.
- Hydrology stage in a new `vernadsky-hydrology` crate: water bodies classified by polar-connected flood fill (the ocean; enclosed components split into seas and lakes by area) with stable centroid-anchored identifiers, and a river network from flow accumulation — height-descending routing with canonical tie-breaks, ice blocks flow, deserts evaporate half of it — traced as source-to-mouth paths that share downstream cells. The stage consumes no randomness. The tools' `water` layer shades by water body kind and a new `rivers` layer highlights the network.
- Biome classification stage in a new `vernadsky-biome` crate: the sixteen-biome mapgen table ported as ordered rule tables (water → mountains → climate) with every threshold on the Vernadsky lattices (°C, %, m), a per-cell boundary dither quantized from the stage-owned `biome.boundary` stream, and a versioned registry (`biomes-v1`) owning identifier ↔ name ↔ category ↔ canonical color. The set is designed to evolve: additions take fresh identifiers, retired identifiers are deprecated but stay interpretable, and unknown identifiers are valid for consumers. The tools' `biome` layer now renders registry colors with a categorical fallback.
- Debug tool in a new `vernadsky-tools` crate: the `render` subcommand rasterizes world layers (height, water, temperature, humidity, precipitation, biome, territory) into PNG maps — climate layers on fixed physical scales so image series stay comparable, absent sections skipped with a warning — and the `dump` subcommand writes a seed-built world as a canonical export file. Worlds can come from an export file or be built from a seed through the current pipeline. Tooling dependencies (`image` with the png feature, `clap`, `anyhow`) live in the tools crate only; the generator crates stay free of them.
- Climate generation stage in a new `vernadsky-climate` crate: per-cell annual-mean temperature, relative humidity, and precipitation, ported from the mapgen prototype and re-derived in physical units (°C, %, mm/year) for the Vernadsky lattices. FastNoiseLite temperature noise is seeded from the stage-owned `climate.temperature` stream; formulas, constants, and their sources are documented in the crate rustdoc; a stage-local golden manifest pins the exports for the core seed set.
- Climate generation parameters in the export contract (params version 1): `GenerationParams` carries stage parameters as canonical integer lattices (`ClimateParams`), the format writer derives the params version from their presence, and the reader accepts versions 0 and 1 while rejecting unknown ones. Version 0 exports remain byte-identical and readable.
- `RngStreams::stream_bytes` in `vernadsky-core`: leading bytes of a named stream without importing the RNG stack.
- Golden seed manifest in `vernadsky-core` tests: a fixed seed set (including boundary values) with pinned content hashes of full exports, a hash sensitivity canary, and a documented regeneration procedure. The synthetic world constructor is now seed-parameterized through its `synthetic.*` RNG streams, making it the reference consumer of the stream strategy.
- Named RNG streams in `vernadsky-core` (`rng-streams-v1`): ChaCha8 streams derived from the world seed and a stage-owned stream name, independent and re-entrant, with locality across stream sets. First runtime dependency: `rand_chacha`.
- `GeographicWorld` schema v0 in `vernadsky-core`: the frozen data contract with typed lattice values and stable identifiers (`content-hash-v1`), a canonical little-endian binary export format with content hashing and a validating reader, and a deterministic synthetic world constructor backing golden round-trip tests.
- Bootstrap crate scaffold: package metadata, dual MIT or Apache-2.0 license, and a committed `Cargo.lock` for reproducible builds.
- CI on GitHub Actions: rustfmt, clippy, rustdoc, and tests on a pinned toolchain, plus a guard against references to private repositories.
- `AGENTS.md` describing scope, core invariants, and dependency policy.

### Changed

- Restructured the repository into a Cargo workspace with a virtual root manifest: the bootstrap crate moved to `crates/core` as `vernadsky-core`, inheriting version, edition, license, MSRV, and publish policy from workspace metadata, with `unsafe_code = "forbid"` applied workspace-wide. No public API existed before, so nothing breaks.
- Adopted Rust edition 2024.
